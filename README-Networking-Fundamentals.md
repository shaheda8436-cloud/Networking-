# Networking Fundamentals 🌐

**Cybersecurity Learning Journey — Module 01**

These beginner-friendly notes summarize my introduction to computer networks, common network types, SOHO networking, and different ways to classify networks.

## 1. What is a network?

A **computer network** is a group of two or more devices connected so they can communicate and share information or resources.

**Examples**
- A phone and laptop connected to the same home Wi-Fi.
- Computers connected in a college laboratory.
- A business connecting computers across its offices.

Networks can use wired connections, wireless connections, or both.

## 2. Common network types

### PAN — Personal Area Network
Connects personal devices over a short distance.

**Example:** A phone connected to Bluetooth earbuds.

### LAN — Local Area Network
Connects devices in a limited area, such as a home, office, school, or laboratory.

**Example:** Computers connected in a college computer lab.

### WLAN — Wireless Local Area Network
A LAN that uses wireless communication, commonly Wi-Fi.

**Example:** A laptop connected to a home router over Wi-Fi.

> LAN describes a local network. WLAN specifically highlights wireless local connectivity.

### CAN — Campus Area Network
Connects multiple LANs across a campus or nearby buildings.

**Example:** A university network connecting academic blocks, a library, and laboratories.

*Context matters: CAN can also mean Controller Area Network in automotive and embedded systems.*

### MAN — Metropolitan Area Network
Connects networks across a city or metropolitan area.

**Example:** An organization linking locations across a city.

### WAN — Wide Area Network
Connects networks over large geographical areas, such as different regions, countries, or continents.

**Example:** A company connecting offices in different countries.

The internet is a global network of interconnected networks and is a well-known example of a WAN-scale internetwork.

## 3. What is SOHO?

**SOHO** stands for **Small Office/Home Office**. It describes a small work environment that needs networking for devices, internet access, and resource sharing.

A typical SOHO setup may include:
- A router or internet gateway.
- Wi-Fi access for laptops and phones.
- A printer or other shared devices.
- Basic security, such as a strong administrator password, WPA2/WPA3 Wi-Fi security, and updated firmware.

**Example:** Someone working from home connects a laptop and printer to a Wi-Fi router.

> SOHO is not a geographical network category like LAN or WAN. It describes an environment; a SOHO setup commonly uses a LAN/WLAN and an internet connection.

## 4. Other ways to classify networks

### By connection method
- **Wired:** Uses cables, commonly Ethernet.
- **Wireless:** Uses technologies such as Wi-Fi or Bluetooth.

### By how resources are shared
- **Client-server:** Servers provide services or resources to client devices.  
  *Example: office computers accessing files on a company server.*
- **Peer-to-peer (P2P):** Devices can share resources directly with one another.  
  *Example: two computers sharing files directly.*

### By access or purpose
- **Internet:** A global system of interconnected networks.
- **Intranet:** A private network or set of services used within an organization.
- **Extranet:** Controlled access that allows authorized external users to use selected organizational resources.

## 5. Quick comparison

| Term | What it describes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| PAN | Personal, short-range connectivity | Phone and Bluetooth earbuds |
| LAN | Network in a limited area | College computer lab |
| WLAN | Wireless local network | Home Wi-Fi |
| CAN | Campus-wide connectivity | University buildings connected |
| MAN | City-scale connectivity | Network linking sites across a city |
| WAN | Large geographical reach | Company offices in multiple countries |
| SOHO | Small-office/home-office environment | Home office with a router and laptop |

These are typical examples, not strict distance limits. Actual networks vary by design and technology.

## 6. Why networking matters in cybersecurity

Networking fundamentals help explain how devices communicate, how services are shared, and where network boundaries exist. They are a foundation for learning:
- IP addresses and ports
- DNS and HTTP/HTTPS
- Routing and network devices
- Network traffic analysis
- Firewalls and secure network configuration

For practical work, use systems and labs you own or have explicit permission to test.

## 7. Quick self-check

1. **What is a computer network?**  
   Connected devices that can communicate and share information or resources.

2. **What does SOHO stand for?**  
   Small Office/Home Office.

3. **Is SOHO the same kind of classification as WAN?**  
   No. SOHO describes an environment; WAN describes broad geographical reach.

4. **Which network type commonly connects Bluetooth earbuds to a phone?**  
   PAN.

5. **What is the difference between LAN and WLAN?**  
   WLAN specifically refers to wireless local connectivity; a LAN may use wired connections, wireless connections, or both.

6. **What is one difference between client-server and peer-to-peer networking?**  
   Client-server uses servers to provide services to clients; peer-to-peer allows devices to share directly.
